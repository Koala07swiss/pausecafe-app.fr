How this calculation works

Caffeine does not vanish all at once: it is absorbed over about an hour, peaks, then declines steadily. This calculator reproduces both phases with a one-compartment model, the standard way pharmacokinetics describes a swallowed substance.

// amount remaining at time t, for a dose D taken at t = 0
C(t) = D × ka / (ka − ke) × ( e−ke·t − e−ka·t )
  • 5 hElimination half-life in a healthy adult
  • ≈ 45 minTime to peak, a consequence of absorption
  • 100 mgSleep alert threshold (EFSA)
  • 400 mgDaily amount of no concern (EFSA)

What the curve shows — and what it doesn't

The line represents the estimated amount of caffeine present in your body, in milligrams. Not a blood concentration, and not a measure of how strongly you feel it. The peak around 45 minutes marks the moment absorption stops outpacing elimination — the effects themselves start well before that, from 15 to 30 minutes in.

The green band marks the zone below 100 mg. EFSA uses this figure as the amount above which caffeine, taken close to bedtime, may lengthen the time it takes to fall asleep and shorten sleep. It is not a hard line: sensitivity varies widely between people.

Why the half-life isn't adjustable here

You will find calculators that let you set your own half-life. We deliberately don't. It depends on factors no form can measure: your CYP1A2 genetic variant, smoking, oral contraception, pregnancy, certain medications. Smoking roughly halves it; oral contraception can double it. Offering a slider would amount to asking you to pick a number at random, then dressing it up as precision.

So we use the population average, 5 hours, while accepting that your own figure may differ considerably. The only honest personalisation comes from observation: comparing your evening caffeine against your actual sleep over several weeks. That is what PauseCafé's insights do, using data from the Health app.

The amounts shown are averages

An espresso can range from 60 to 120 mg depending on the grind, the variety and the extraction time. The values offered here are typical orders of magnitude. For accurate tracking, PauseCafé records the real content of every drink, including your own custom ones.

Frequently asked questions

How much caffeine is left in my body at bedtime?

It depends on how much you drank, when, and on your metabolism. With an average half-life of 5 hours, an 80 mg espresso taken at 3pm leaves about 27 mg by 11pm; the same coffee at 6pm leaves 41 mg. This calculator adds up what remains from each of your drinks at your exact bedtime.

What is the half-life of caffeine?

It is the time your body takes to clear half the caffeine present. In a healthy adult it averages 5 hours, with a documented range of 1.5 to 9.5 hours according to the Institute of Medicine. After 5 hours half the dose remains, after 10 hours a quarter, after 15 hours an eighth.

Why can't I adjust my half-life here?

Because no form can measure it. It depends on your CYP1A2 genetic variant, smoking, oral contraception, pregnancy or certain medications. Smoking roughly halves it; oral contraception can double it. Offering a slider would amount to asking you to pick a number at random, then dressing it up as precision. So we use the population average of 5 hours, accepting that your own figure may differ.

At what level does caffeine interfere with sleep?

EFSA uses 100 mg as the threshold above which caffeine taken close to bedtime may lengthen the time it takes to fall asleep and shorten sleep. It is not a hard line: sensitivity varies widely. Some people sleep after an evening espresso; others are affected by a 4pm tea.

How much caffeine per day is reasonable?

EFSA considers 400 mg per day to be of no concern for healthy adults, and 200 mg for a single dose. For pregnant or breastfeeding women the figure drops to 200 mg per day. These are population benchmarks, not individual limits.

Can you speed up caffeine elimination?

No, no reliable method speeds it up. Drinking water, walking or taking a shower may improve how you feel, but only time actually clears caffeine. The one effective strategy is to move your intake earlier in the day.

Does decaf coffee contain caffeine?

Yes, but very little: around 12 mg per decaf espresso, against 80 mg for a regular one. At that level the impact on sleep is negligible for the vast majority of people.

⚕️ Important. This calculator is for information and general wellbeing only. It is not medical advice, a diagnosis or a prescription. The effects of caffeine vary greatly from one person to another. If you have questions about your intake, trouble sleeping, or any concern about pregnancy, medical treatment or a health condition, speak to a healthcare professional.
