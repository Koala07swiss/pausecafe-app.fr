How much caffeine is in an energy drink?

80 mg

For a standard 250 ml can, or 32 mg per 100 ml. That is the equivalent of an espresso. The large 500 ml can holds 160 mg, and that is where the count slips.

Why such a wide range?

Four factors explain almost all of it.

Can size, and little else. This is the peculiarity of the category: the concentration is nearly always the same, 32 mg per 100 ml. What changes is the container. A large can is two doses, not one.

Concentrated shots. Sold in small formats, they often hold 200 mg in 60 ml — the EFSA single-dose mark, in two mouthfuls.

Guarana, which adds up. Some formulas add guarana, whose caffeine does not always appear in the stated total. Real content can then exceed the label.

Regulatory wording. In Europe, any drink above 150 mg per litre must carry the notice « High caffeine content ». It is a useful marker when the exact figure is not given.

A can equals how many coffees?

A standard can equals one espresso. A large 500 ml can equals two espressos, or one and a half filter coffees.

The gap with coffee is not in the dose but in the way it is drunk: an espresso goes down in a minute, a can over half an hour, often alongside something else.

And at bedtime, how much is left?

An 80 mg can at 5 pm still leaves about 34 mg at 11 pm. A large can at the same hour leaves 68, two thirds of the 100 mg mark EFSA uses for sleep.

This decline follows caffeine's half-life, on average 5 hours in a healthy adult.

Frequently asked

Does an energy drink have more caffeine than coffee?

Not at comparable volume, but often more in total. A 250 ml can holds 80 mg, like an espresso. A 500 ml can holds 160 mg, more than a mug of filter coffee.

What does « high caffeine content » mean on the label?

It is a mandatory notice in Europe for any drink above 150 mg of caffeine per litre. It must come with the exact content and the wording « Not recommended for children or pregnant or breast-feeding women ».

Does guarana count in the stated caffeine?

Not always. Guarana naturally contains caffeine, sometimes accounted for separately from added caffeine. Where a formula contains it, real content can exceed the printed figure.

Are energy shots more concentrated?

Far more. A 60 ml shot commonly holds 200 mg, the EFSA single-dose mark in a few mouthfuls. The small format misleads: small does not mean light.

Can you mix energy drinks and coffee?

Nothing forbids it, but the total climbs fast: a coffee in the morning, two cans through the day, and the 400 mg mark is already close. The issue is not the mix, it is the daily total.
