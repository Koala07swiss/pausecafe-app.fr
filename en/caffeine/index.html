Three common reading mistakes

Confusing concentration with quantity. Espresso is the most concentrated drink, but not the one delivering the most caffeine: you drink 30 ml of it against 240 for a filter coffee. What counts for your body is the total swallowed.

Forgetting the real size. A large mug is not a cup, a double espresso is not an espresso, a large can is not a can. It is the factor that most distorts calculations, and one that almost no table accounts for.

Assuming a gentle drink is a light one. A matcha latte holds as much caffeine as a plain matcha — milk adds volume, not caffeine. A green tea drunk four times in an afternoon equals a filter coffee.

Frequently asked

Which drink has the most caffeine?

Among everyday drinks, the 500 ml energy can leads with 160 mg, ahead of the large mug of filter coffee at 158 mg and the double espresso at 154 mg. Per unit of volume, espresso stays the most concentrated: about 250 mg per 100 ml.

Does espresso have more caffeine than filter coffee?

No, contrary to a widespread belief. A 30 ml espresso brings 77 mg, a 240 ml mug of filter brings 108. Espresso is more concentrated, but the volume is eight times smaller.

How much caffeine a day is reasonable?

EFSA considers 400 mg a day raises no concern for a healthy adult, and 200 mg for a single dose. For pregnant or breast-feeding women the mark drops to 200 mg a day. These are population figures, not individual limits.

Why do figures vary so much between sources?

Because the reference portion changes from one source to another, and because preparation genuinely matters. A cup can be 150 or 350 ml, an espresso pulled from arabica or robusta. The ranges here reflect that variability instead of hiding it.
